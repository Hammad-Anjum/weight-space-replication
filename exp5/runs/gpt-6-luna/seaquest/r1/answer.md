The first layer encodes the sub’s approximate position, the diver signal’s horizontal and vertical moments (enemy cells cancel between reward and danger channels), enemy presence, oxygen in the first three gauge cells, and whether the sub is near the surface. The second layer turns the position differences into directional preferences, adds a modest firing preference when danger is present, and strongly favors surfacing when oxygen is low. A near-surface downward bias helps avoid surfacing before the sub has collected divers.
